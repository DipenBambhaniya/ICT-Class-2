Mission Mode Projects & Digital Platforms

Mission Mode Projects (MMPs) are the individually funded, milestone-driven e-governance projects that make up the National e-Governance Plan (NeGP). They are grouped as Central, State or Integrated depending on which level of government owns them, and they increasingly run on shared national digital platforms — the Digitization India Platform, DigiLocker, GI Cloud "MeghRaj" — that e-Kranti (NeGP 2.0) pushes every MMP to reuse rather than reinvent.

Key points

Mission Mode Project (MMP)
An individual e-governance project under NeGP with defined objectives, scope, timelines, and measurable service-delivery outcomes — "mission mode" meaning it has clear milestones and accountable ownership, not an open-ended IT initiative.
Central MMPs
Owned and implemented by union ministries/departments — e.g. Income Tax (e-filing), Passport Seva, MCA21 (Ministry of Corporate Affairs), Central Excise, Banking, Insurance, Pension.
State MMPs
Owned and implemented by individual state governments — e.g. Land Records (Bhoomi in Karnataka and similar systems elsewhere), e-District, Commercial Taxes, Employment Exchange, Municipalities, Police, Treasuries.
Integrated MMPs
Span both central and state levels and need coordinated delivery across them — e.g. e-Courts, Common Services Centres (CSC), UID/Aadhaar, GST, e-Trade, National e-Governance Service Delivery Gateway.
Digitization India Platform (DIP)
A national framework/platform for converting government records, documents and archives from physical/scanned form into structured, searchable digital data that departments and MMPs can reuse.
DigiLocker & GI Cloud "MeghRaj"
DigiLocker gives every citizen a secure digital document space (issued documents, eSign, self-uploaded documents); MeghRaj is India's Government Community Cloud strategy — a panel of empanelled cloud service providers that departments and MMPs use instead of each building its own data centre.

Example

A state government launching a new "e-District" MMP (State category)
for issuing income/caste/domicile certificates online:

  1. Citizen records already digitized under the Digitization India
     Platform (old paper land/revenue records converted to searchable
     digital data) are reused instead of re-keying data from scratch.
  2. The application is hosted on an empanelled cloud provider under
     GI Cloud "MeghRaj" instead of the state building its own data
     centre — faster rollout, lower cost, easier scaling at peak load.
  3. Once issued, the certificate is pushed straight into the citizen's
     DigiLocker account, digitally signed, so it can be reused later
     (e.g. as proof for a Central MMP like a passport application)
     without asking the citizen to submit paper copies again.

This single State MMP therefore depends on one Integrated MMP-style
identity layer (Aadhaar-based authentication) and two shared national
platforms (DIP, MeghRaj) plus DigiLocker for delivery — exactly the
kind of reuse e-Kranti's "Transformation and not Translation"
principle is meant to encourage.
Common exam traps

Don't sort MMPs by which level of government a citizen interacts with — sort by which level of government owns and funds the project. e-Courts, CSC, UID/Aadhaar and GST are Integrated MMPs because their delivery genuinely spans centre and state, not because citizens use them everywhere. GI Cloud "MeghRaj" is a Government Community Cloud strategy (empanelled providers serving government under government-specific security/compliance terms) — it is not a single public cloud product citizens can sign up for. DigiLocker and the Digitization India Platform are related but distinct: DIP digitizes government's own back-office records; DigiLocker is the citizen-facing locker where issued/self-uploaded documents live. e-Kranti is NeGP 2.0 (an expansion/upgrade of NeGP's MMP approach), not a completely separate program.

Practice MCQs

Q1. A Mission Mode Project (MMP) under the National e-Governance Plan is best defined as:

  1. Any government IT purchase, regardless of scope or accountability
  2. An individual e-governance project with defined objectives, scope, timelines and measurable service-delivery outcomes, owned by a specific ministry, department or state
  3. A private-sector software product with no government involvement
  4. A one-time training workshop for government employees
Show answer

Answer: B. "Mission mode" specifically means clear milestones, defined ownership and measurable outcomes, not an open-ended or unscoped IT effort.

Q2. Which of the following is classified as a Central Mission Mode Project?

  1. Land Records
  2. Commercial Taxes
  3. Passport Seva
  4. e-Courts
Show answer

Answer: C. Passport Seva is implemented by a union ministry (Ministry of External Affairs), making it a Central MMP; Land Records and Commercial Taxes are State MMPs, and e-Courts is Integrated.

Q3. UID/Aadhaar and GST are examples of which category of Mission Mode Project?

  1. Central MMP only
  2. State MMP only
  3. Integrated MMP, since their implementation spans both central and state governments
  4. They are not MMPs at all
Show answer

Answer: C. Integrated MMPs require coordinated delivery across both central and state levels, which is exactly the case for Aadhaar-based identity and GST.

Q4. GI Cloud, branded "MeghRaj", is best described as:

  1. A public commercial cloud service sold directly to citizens
  2. India's Government Community Cloud strategy — a panel of empanelled cloud service providers that government departments use to host applications faster, at lower cost and with government-appropriate security/compliance
  3. A physical data centre built and owned by a single private telecom company
  4. An offline document-storage cabinet used in government offices
Show answer

Answer: B. MeghRaj is a community-cloud model for government use, built on empanelled providers, not a citizen-facing commercial cloud product.

Q5. e-Kranti, described as "NeGP 2.0", is guided by the principle of:

  1. "Translation and not Transformation" — simply moving paper forms online unchanged
  2. "Transformation and not Translation" — redesigning government processes for genuinely better service delivery rather than just digitizing the old paper process as-is
  3. Replacing all Mission Mode Projects with a single unrelated initiative
  4. Restricting e-governance to only central government departments
Show answer

Answer: B. e-Kranti's guiding principle is process transformation, not merely translating an existing paper-based workflow into a digital form.
