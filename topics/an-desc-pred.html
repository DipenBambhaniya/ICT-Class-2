Descriptive & Predictive Analytics

Analytics maturity progresses from describing the past to forecasting the future to recommending action. Descriptive analytics summarizes what happened; predictive analytics forecasts what's likely to happen next.

Key points

Descriptive analytics
"What happened?" — dashboards, KPI reports, summary statistics (mean, total, trend) over historical/current data.
Diagnostic analytics
"Why did it happen?" — drilling into causes, correlations, root-cause analysis behind an observed pattern.
Predictive analytics
"What is likely to happen?" — regression/classification models trained on historical, LABELED data to forecast future/unknown outcomes.
Prescriptive analytics
"What should we do?" — the most advanced tier, recommending or optimizing specific actions based on predictions.
Maturity progression
Descriptive → Diagnostic → Predictive → Prescriptive: increasing complexity and business value, each often building on the last.

Diagram

Example

Descriptive:  "Sales dropped 15% in the North region last month."
Diagnostic:   "The drop correlates with a competitor opening
               two new stores in that region."
Predictive:   "Given current trends, North region sales are
               forecast to drop a further 5% next quarter
               unless action is taken."
Prescriptive: "Recommend a targeted 10% promotion in the North
               region, projected to recover ~60% of the loss."
Common exam traps

Descriptive = backward-looking summary; predictive = forward-looking forecast — don't swap these. Predictive models output a PROBABILITY/estimate, not a certainty. Diagnostic sits between descriptive and predictive, focused on causes ("why"), not forecasts. Prescriptive analytics is the most advanced tier — it RECOMMENDS action, going beyond just predicting an outcome.

Practice MCQs

Q1. Descriptive analytics primarily answers:

  1. What will happen next?
  2. What happened?
  3. Why did it happen?
  4. What should we do?
Show answer

Answer: B. Descriptive analytics summarizes historical/current data.

Q2. Predictive analytics primarily answers:

  1. What happened?
  2. What is likely to happen in the future?
  3. How is data stored?
  4. How do we visualize data?
Show answer

Answer: B. Uses historical patterns to forecast unknown/future outcomes.

Q3. Diagnostic analytics focuses on:

  1. Predicting future outcomes
  2. Understanding WHY something happened
  3. Automating a decision
  4. Storing raw data
Show answer

Answer: B. Drills into causes and correlations behind an observed pattern.

Q4. Prescriptive analytics goes beyond prediction by:

  1. Only describing past performance
  2. Recommending specific actions to achieve a desired outcome
  3. Storing data in a warehouse
  4. Cleaning raw data
Show answer

Answer: B. Often uses optimization/simulation on top of predictions.

Q5. A dashboard of last month's total revenue and active users is an example of:

  1. Predictive analytics
  2. Descriptive analytics
  3. Prescriptive analytics
  4. Not analytics at all
Show answer

Answer: B. Summarizing current/historical KPIs is descriptive.
