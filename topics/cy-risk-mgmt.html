IT Security & Risk Management

Risk management turns threats and vulnerabilities into a structured, ongoing process: identify, assess, treat, monitor. Incident response handles what happens when prevention fails. In India, CERT-In coordinates this at a national level as the nodal cybersecurity incident-response agency.

Watch

▶ Watch: "IT Risk Manager Explained: Identifying, Assessing and Mitigating IT Risks" (YouTube)

Key points

Risk management cycle
Identify → assess (likelihood × impact) → treat (avoid, mitigate, transfer, or accept) → continuously monitor.
Risk treatment options
Avoid (eliminate), mitigate (reduce), transfer (e.g. insurance), or accept (a conscious, documented decision) — chosen by cost/benefit.
Incident response phases
Preparation → detection/identification → containment → eradication → recovery → lessons learned.
CERT-In
India's national nodal agency for cybersecurity incidents — issues advisories, collects/analyzes incident data, coordinates national response.
Defense in depth
Layering multiple controls (technical, procedural, awareness training) so no single failure causes full compromise.

Diagram

Example

A bank assessing ransomware risk:
  Identify:  legacy file server running unpatched software (vulnerability);
             ransomware gangs actively targeting the finance sector (threat)
  Assess:    High likelihood + high impact (customer data, regulatory fines,
             reputational damage) -> flagged as a top-priority risk
  Treat:     Mitigate — patch the server, segment it from the main network,
             deploy endpoint detection; Transfer — buy cyber insurance
  Monitor:   SOC/SIEM continuously watches for suspicious activity

If an incident occurs anyway: contain (isolate the infected segment),
eradicate (remove the malware), recover (restore from tested backups),
and — since this is a significant incident — report it as required to
CERT-In, which can issue a broader advisory if the threat is widespread.
Common exam traps

Risk ACCEPTANCE is a conscious, documented decision — not the same as ignoring a risk. CERT-In is India's NATIONAL nodal incident-response agency (not a private vendor). Defense in depth means LAYERED controls, not one strong control. Preventive controls (firewalls) stop incidents before they happen; detective controls (IDS, SIEM) find them during/after; corrective controls (backups, patching) fix things afterward — these three are often confused.

Practice MCQs

Q1. The risk management process typically follows the general sequence:

  1. Mitigate, then identify, then assess, with no monitoring
  2. Identify risks, assess/analyze them, treat/mitigate them, and continuously monitor/review
  3. Only monitor, with no other steps
  4. Assess risks only once, at the very start of an organization's existence, with no further review
Show answer

Answer: B. Risk management is an ongoing cycle, not a one-time activity, since the threat landscape and organization both change over time.

Q2. CERT-In (Indian Computer Emergency Response Team) functions as:

  1. A private for-profit antivirus vendor
  2. India's national nodal agency for responding to cybersecurity incidents, issuing advisories, and coordinating incident response
  3. A branch of a foreign government with no role in India
  4. A hardware manufacturer
Show answer

Answer: B. CERT-In operates under India's Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) as the national incident response coordinator.

Q3. The typical phases of incident response include:

  1. Only a single phase: cleanup
  2. Preparation, detection/identification, containment, eradication, recovery, and lessons learned/post-incident review
  3. Only detection, with no further action taken
  4. Only occurring for incidents affecting external customers, never internal systems
Show answer

Answer: B. This structured lifecycle helps ensure incidents are handled thoroughly, not just superficially patched.

Q4. Defense in depth, as an IT security risk management strategy, means:

  1. Relying on a single very strong security control
  2. Layering multiple, complementary security controls so that the failure of any one control doesn't lead to a full compromise
  3. Only applying security measures at the network perimeter, with nothing internal
  4. Ignoring physical security entirely
Show answer

Answer: B. This layered approach acknowledges that no single control is perfect, so redundancy across layers reduces overall risk.

Q5. Risk treatment (mitigation) options generally include:

  1. Only one option: fully eliminate every risk with no other choice
  2. Avoiding, mitigating/reducing, transferring (e.g., via insurance), or accepting a given risk, depending on its severity and the cost of addressing it
  3. Ignoring all risks regardless of severity
  4. Only purchasing new hardware, with no other possible response
Show answer

Answer: B. Organizations choose among these strategies based on a cost-benefit analysis of each risk.
