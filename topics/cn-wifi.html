Basics of Wi-Fi

802.11 adapts LAN concepts to a shared, broadcast, half-duplex radio medium. Because a station can't reliably detect a collision while transmitting, Wi-Fi avoids collisions rather than detecting them, and encryption is essential rather than optional.

Key points

CSMA/CA
Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision AVOIDANCE — sense, defer, random backoff, then transmit. RTS/CTS mitigates the hidden terminal problem.
Hidden terminal problem
Two stations both in range of an AP but not of each other may transmit simultaneously, colliding at the AP without either sensing the other.
AP / SSID / BSS
An Access Point bridges wireless clients (infrastructure mode). SSID is the network name. A BSS is one AP plus its associated clients; an ESS links multiple BSSs under one SSID.
Security
WEP is broken; WPA2 is the long-standing baseline; WPA3 adds SAE (resists offline password-guessing) — always prefer WPA2/WPA3 over open or WEP networks.
Bands & interference
2.4 GHz: longer range, more interference, fewer non-overlapping channels (1/6/11). 5/6 GHz: shorter range, less congestion, more channels/throughput.

Diagram

Example

RTS/CTS exchange mitigating the hidden terminal problem:

Station A -> AP:  RTS (Request to Send)
AP        -> ALL: CTS (Clear to Send) -- heard by BOTH A and B
Station B: hears the CTS, knows the medium is reserved, stays silent
Station A -> AP:  sends data safely, no collision from B
Common exam traps

Wi-Fi uses CSMA/CA, NOT CSMA/CD (collision detection doesn't work well on a half-duplex radio link). WEP is insecure and should never be used. MAC filtering and SSID hiding are weak, easily-bypassed measures — not substitutes for WPA2/WPA3 encryption. 5 GHz has MORE channels/throughput but SHORTER range than 2.4 GHz, not the reverse.

Practice MCQs

Q1. Wi-Fi is standardized under which IEEE family?

  1. 802.3
  2. 802.11
  3. 802.15
  4. 802.1
Show answer

Answer: B. Amendments include 802.11n, ac, ax (Wi-Fi 6).

Q2. Wi-Fi's medium access method is:

  1. CSMA/CD, exactly like Ethernet
  2. CSMA/CA (Collision Avoidance)
  3. Pure ALOHA with no carrier sensing
  4. Token passing
Show answer

Answer: B. A station cannot reliably detect a collision while transmitting on a half-duplex radio link.

Q3. The hidden terminal problem occurs when:

  1. Two stations in range of an AP but not of each other may collide at the AP
  2. All stations are always in range of each other
  3. A station has no MAC address
  4. The AP is physically hidden
Show answer

Answer: A. RTS/CTS mitigates this.

Q4. Which is the most secure choice among these?

  1. WEP
  2. Open network (no password)
  3. WPA2/WPA3
  4. MAC filtering alone
Show answer

Answer: C. WEP is broken; MAC filtering/SSID hiding alone are weak.

Q5. Compared to 2.4 GHz, the 5 GHz Wi-Fi band generally offers:

  1. Longer range and better wall penetration
  2. Shorter range but more channels and less congestion
  3. Identical characteristics
  4. No support for higher data rates
Show answer

Answer: B. This is why dual-band routers offer both.
