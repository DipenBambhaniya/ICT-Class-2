Enterprise Systems, EDI/ERP & IT Governance

EDI moves structured documents BETWEEN organizations; ERP integrates processes WITHIN one organization; Citizen Relationship Management applies CRM thinking to government-citizen interactions. IT/ITeS usage policy and the COBIT5 governance framework provide the structure that keeps all of this aligned, accountable, and well-managed.

Key points

EDI
Structured, standardized (e.g. ANSI X12, EDIFACT) electronic exchange of business documents between organizations — faster, cheaper, fewer errors than paper.
ERP
Integrated suite managing core processes (finance, HR, supply chain, manufacturing) within one organization on a shared data model.
Citizen Relationship Management
CRM principles applied to government — tracking citizen service requests/grievances for responsive, accountable service delivery.
COBIT5's 5 principles
Meet stakeholder needs · cover the enterprise end-to-end · apply a single integrated framework · enable a holistic approach · separate governance from management.

Diagram

Example

A government hospital's digital transformation:
  ERP:  Integrates finance, HR (staff scheduling), and pharmacy inventory
        into one system — a doctor's prescription automatically updates
        pharmacy stock levels, no manual re-entry needed
  EDI:  The hospital's ERP automatically sends a structured purchase
        order to a medical-supplies vendor's system when stock is low —
        no phone calls or paper forms
  CzRM: Patient complaints/feedback are logged centrally and routed to
        the right department, with status trackable by the patient
  Governance: The hospital's IT usage policy (informed by COBIT5) defines
        who can access patient records, and the hospital board reviews
        IT risk quarterly — separating governance (board) from
        management (IT department's day-to-day execution)
Common exam traps

EDI = BETWEEN organizations; ERP = WITHIN one organization — a frequently confused pair. COBIT5's "separating governance from management" means governance SETS DIRECTION/oversees, management EXECUTES — not that the two are unrelated. COBIT5 is a governance FRAMEWORK, not a piece of software or a single product.

Practice MCQs

Q1. EDI (Electronic Data Interchange) is best defined as:

  1. A social media messaging protocol
  2. The structured, standardized electronic exchange of business documents (like purchase orders, invoices) directly between organizations' computer systems, without manual re-entry
  3. A type of physical mail delivery service
  4. A programming language used only for web development
Show answer

Answer: B. EDI replaces manual, paper-based document exchange with direct, structured, computer-to-computer data transfer.

Q2. ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) systems are best described as:

  1. A single-purpose tool that only manages email
  2. Integrated software suites that manage and coordinate an organization's core business processes (finance, HR, supply chain, manufacturing, etc.) within a unified system
  3. A type of external EDI-only messaging network
  4. A tool used exclusively for graphic design
Show answer

Answer: B. ERP's defining trait is integration — bringing together previously siloed business functions into one coordinated system.

Q3. Citizen Relationship Management (CzRM), applying CRM concepts to government, primarily focuses on:

  1. Managing a private company's sales pipeline with no government relevance
  2. Systematically tracking and managing citizens' interactions, service requests, and grievances with government to improve service quality and responsiveness
  3. Only managing government employees' internal HR records
  4. A synonym for ERP with no distinction
Show answer

Answer: B. CzRM adapts private-sector customer relationship management principles to the government-citizen relationship, emphasizing service quality and responsiveness.

Q4. COBIT5 (Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies) is best described as:

  1. A specific software product sold by a single vendor
  2. A widely recognized framework for the governance and management of enterprise IT, helping align IT activities with business goals and manage IT-related risk
  3. A programming language for building enterprise applications
  4. A type of hardware networking cable
Show answer

Answer: B. COBIT5 is a governance/management framework, not a specific product, providing structured guidance for enterprise IT governance.

Q5. COBIT5's principle of 'separating governance from management' distinguishes between:

  1. Two identical activities with no meaningful distinction
  2. Governance (setting direction, evaluating options, monitoring performance — typically a board-level responsibility) and management (planning, building, running, and monitoring activities in line with that direction — typically an executive-level responsibility)
  3. Only IT staff performing governance, with no role in management ever
  4. A distinction that applies only to non-IT parts of an organization
Show answer

Answer: B. This governance-vs-management distinction is a foundational COBIT5 concept, clarifying different levels of accountability within an organization.
