E-Governance Models & Digital India

Commerce (transactions), Business (broader operations) and Governance (public administration) each play a distinct role in Digital India's push to deliver services electronically. Interaction models — G2G, C2C, C2B, C2G, B2C and more — describe WHO is exchanging value/information with whom, and collaborative APIs/platforms are how India builds shared digital infrastructure that many applications can be built on.

Key points

Commerce vs Business vs Governance
Commerce = buying/selling transactions. Business = the broader set of an organization's processes (beyond just transactions). Governance = how government exercises authority and delivers public services.
Interaction models
G2G (government-government), G2C/C2G (government-citizen), G2B/C2B (government/citizen-business), C2C (citizen-citizen), B2C (business-citizen) — each names WHO initiates and WHO receives.
Digital India pillars
Digital infrastructure as a core utility, governance & services on demand, and digital empowerment of citizens.
Collaborative APIs/platforms
India Stack (Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker) exposes open APIs so businesses/developers can build on shared government digital infrastructure — UMANG/DigiLocker are examples of resulting collaborative platforms.

Diagram

Example

A startup building a lending app on India Stack:
  1. Uses the Aadhaar API (collaborative API) to verify a customer's identity
  2. Uses UPI (collaborative API) to disburse the loan directly to the
     customer's bank account
  3. The customer later shares a digitally-signed income certificate from
     DigiLocker (collaborative platform) to support their application

Interaction models at play:
  - Citizen <-> Business (C2B / B2C): the customer applying for and
    receiving the loan
  - Government <-> Business (G2B): the startup using government-provided
    Aadhaar/UPI infrastructure under regulatory terms
  - Government <-> Citizen (G2C): DigiLocker itself is a G2C service the
    citizen used earlier to obtain the digital certificate
Common exam traps

Commerce (transactions) is narrower than Business (all operations), which is narrower in scope than Governance (public authority/service delivery) — don't treat the three as synonyms. G2G is government-to-government (NOT citizen-facing at all). C2C can still involve a business platform in the middle (like a marketplace app) — the interaction model names who the value flows BETWEEN, not who built the software.

Practice MCQs

Q1. In the C/B/G framework, 'Governance (G)' refers to:

  1. Only private company management
  2. How government exercises authority and delivers public services, increasingly through digital means (e-Governance) to improve efficiency, transparency and citizen engagement
  3. A synonym for commerce with no distinction
  4. Only judicial court proceedings
Show answer

Answer: B. Governance encompasses the exercise of public authority and administration, a role distinct from private commerce/business.

Q2. G2G (Government-to-Government) e-governance interactions refer to:

  1. Interactions between a government and its private-sector vendors only
  2. Electronic interactions/data sharing between different government departments, agencies, or levels of government (e.g., state to central)
  3. Interactions solely between citizens and government
  4. A type of business-to-business e-commerce
Show answer

Answer: B. G2G improves coordination and information sharing across the machinery of government itself.

Q3. A 'collaborative API' in the context of Digital India/e-governance refers to:

  1. An API that only one single organization is ever allowed to use
  2. A programmatic interface that lets multiple stakeholders (government, businesses, developers) securely access and build upon a shared digital service or dataset
  3. A physical hardware connector
  4. A type of encryption algorithm
Show answer

Answer: B. APIs like those in India Stack (Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker) enable an ecosystem of third-party applications to be built on shared government digital infrastructure.

Q4. UMANG (Unified Mobile Application for New-age Governance) is an example of:

  1. A single-purpose private banking app
  2. A collaborative digital platform that integrates multiple central and state government services into a single mobile application for citizens
  3. A hardware device for government offices
  4. A programming language used to build government websites
Show answer

Answer: B. UMANG exemplifies the collaborative digital platform model, consolidating many government services into one citizen-facing app.

Q5. B2C (Business-to-Consumer/Citizen) interactions refer to:

  1. Businesses interacting only with other businesses
  2. A business selling goods/services directly to individual citizens/consumers, typically the most familiar e-commerce model
  3. Government departments interacting with each other
  4. Only citizens interacting with other citizens
Show answer

Answer: B. B2C is the most common e-commerce model, e.g., an online retailer selling directly to individual customers.
