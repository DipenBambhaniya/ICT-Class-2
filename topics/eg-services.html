Digital Services, Payments & Ethics

e-Learning (SWAYAM), digital payment modes (UPI, IMPS, NEFT/RTGS, USSD, AePS, wallets), and digital libraries (NDLI) are concrete citizen-facing digital services. Public safety applications, ethics, and social responsibility ensure these services genuinely reach — and don't harm — the whole population, not just the already digitally advantaged.

Key points

e-Learning
SWAYAM (free MOOCs) and similar platforms broaden access to education beyond geographic/scheduling constraints.
Digital payment modes
UPI/IMPS (instant), NEFT (batched)/RTGS (real-time, high-value), USSD *99# (works on any phone, no internet), AePS (biometric, no card/PIN needed), mobile wallets.
Digital libraries
NDLI aggregates searchable digital educational content, accessible remotely, at scale, often multilingual.
Ethics & social responsibility
Fairness, transparency, privacy, and deliberately avoiding widening the digital divide — inclusion must be designed in, not assumed.

Diagram

Example

A financial-inclusion drive in a low-connectivity district:
  - A farmer with only a basic phone withdraws cash using AePS
    (fingerprint at a local banking correspondent, no card needed)
  - A shopkeeper with a smartphone accepts UPI payments from customers
  - A migrant worker without a smartphone sends money home using
    USSD *99# banking over a basic cellular signal
  - Local students access SWAYAM courses and NDLI e-books at a shared
    village digital-access center for those without personal devices

Ethics/social responsibility check: the drive is judged not just by how
much technology was deployed, but by whether previously excluded people
(low literacy, no smartphone, no nearby bank branch) can now actually
transact, learn, and access services — not only the already-connected.
Common exam traps

RTGS = real-time, individual settlement, typically for HIGH-value transfers; NEFT = batched settlement, broader range of values — don't swap these. USSD-based banking works WITHOUT internet, on any phone — a common point of confusion with app-based UPI. Ethics/social responsibility isn't a one-time checklist — it requires ongoing monitoring that services actually reach underserved populations, not just that they technically exist.

Practice MCQs

Q1. UPI (Unified Payments Interface) is best described as:

  1. A physical payment card issued by a single bank
  2. A real-time payment system enabling instant bank-to-bank money transfers via a mobile application, using a simplified interface (e.g., a virtual payment address)
  3. A cryptocurrency unrelated to traditional banking
  4. A service available only for business-to-business transactions, never individuals
Show answer

Answer: B. UPI has become one of India's most widely used digital payment mechanisms, enabling instant, simple bank-to-bank transfers.

Q2. USSD-based mobile banking (like India's *99# service) is particularly valuable because it:

  1. Requires an expensive smartphone and high-speed internet connection
  2. Enables basic banking/payment transactions from any mobile phone (including basic feature phones) without requiring internet access, using simple USSD codes
  3. Only works on the very newest smartphone models
  4. Cannot be used to check account balances or make transfers
Show answer

Answer: B. USSD-based services extend digital payment access to users without smartphones or reliable internet connectivity, supporting financial inclusion.

Q3. The Aadhaar Enabled Payment System (AePS) allows a user to:

  1. Make payments only using a physical debit card with no biometric involvement
  2. Perform basic banking transactions (like cash withdrawal or balance inquiry) using their Aadhaar number and biometric authentication (like a fingerprint), without needing a card or PIN
  3. Only be used by users who have a smartphone with internet access
  4. Access banking services only in major metropolitan cities
Show answer

Answer: B. AePS uses Aadhaar-linked biometric authentication to enable banking access, particularly valuable for extending services to underserved populations.

Q4. A digital library, such as India's National Digital Library (NDLI), primarily provides:

  1. Only physical books with no digital access
  2. A centralized, searchable, online repository of digital educational content (books, articles, videos, etc.) accessible to users remotely
  3. A service exclusively for university faculty, with no student access
  4. Only content in a single specific language with no multilingual support
Show answer

Answer: B. Digital libraries aggregate and provide remote, searchable access to educational content, expanding access beyond physical library locations.

Q5. Social responsibility, as it relates to digital government initiatives, involves:

  1. Ignoring the needs of digitally underserved or marginalized populations entirely
  2. Considering the broader societal impact of digital initiatives, including ensuring inclusive access and avoiding exacerbating existing inequalities (like the digital divide)
  3. A concept with no practical bearing on how digital services should be designed
  4. Only benefiting citizens who are already digitally advantaged
Show answer

Answer: B. Socially responsible digital governance actively considers and works to mitigate the risk of leaving underserved populations further behind.
