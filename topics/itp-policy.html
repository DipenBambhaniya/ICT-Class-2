E-Governance Policies & Gujarat IT/ITeS Policy 2022-27

National policy sets the direction e-governance projects follow — NeGP's founding principles, Digital India's three vision areas, and supporting policies like the National Data Sharing and Accessibility Policy. At the state level, Gujarat's IT/ITeS Policy 2022-27 translates that direction into concrete investment: incentives designed to make Gujarat a preferred hub for IT/ITeS, data centres, Global Capability Centres and emerging technologies.

Key points

NeGP's guiding principles
The National e-Governance Plan (2006) was built on principles such as a centralized initiative with decentralized implementation, ownership of MMPs by the concerned ministry/department/state, common support infrastructure to be shared across projects, and public-private partnership wherever it improved service delivery.
Digital India's three vision areas
(1) Digital infrastructure as a core utility to every citizen, (2) governance and services on demand, (3) digital empowerment of citizens — Digital India (2015) builds on and expands NeGP's MMP-based approach.
National Data Sharing and Accessibility Policy (NDSAP)
A 2012 policy framework encouraging government departments to proactively share non-sensitive data they hold (e.g. via the Open Government Data platform) to promote transparency and enable data-driven applications.
Gujarat IT/ITeS Policy 2022-27 — objectives
Attract fresh IT/ITeS investment into Gujarat, generate large-scale skilled employment, and position the state as a preferred destination for IT/ITeS companies, Global Capability Centres (GCCs), data centres and emerging-technology firms.
Gujarat IT/ITeS Policy 2022-27 — incentive categories
Capital subsidy on eligible investment, support for GIDC/IT-park infrastructure, quality-certification and patent-filing support, skill-development assistance, special incentives for data centres and GCCs, dedicated incentives for AI/emerging-tech and cybersecurity companies, and single-window clearance to speed up approvals.

Example

A multinational bank deciding where to set up a new Global Capability
Centre (GCC) evaluates Gujarat under the IT/ITeS Policy 2022-27:

  1. Capital subsidy on the fit-out/investment reduces upfront cost.
  2. GIDC/IT-park infrastructure support gives ready-built space instead
     of the company needing to develop land itself.
  3. Skill-development assistance helps fund training local graduates
     to the specific skills the GCC needs.
  4. Because the centre also plans an in-house cybersecurity operations
     function, it separately qualifies for the policy's dedicated
     cybersecurity-company incentives.
  5. Single-window clearance means the company deals with one nodal
     process instead of separately approaching each department for
     land, power, and registration approvals.

None of this replaces national programs — the GCC's own applications
may still be hosted on GI Cloud "MeghRaj" and will still have to
comply with the IT Act and DPDP Act — but the state policy is what
made Gujarat, specifically, the financially attractive choice.
Common exam traps

NeGP (2006) and Digital India (2015) are not the same program — Digital India expanded on NeGP's Mission Mode Project approach with a broader three-part vision, it did not replace or predate it. The National Data Sharing and Accessibility Policy governs how government shares its own non-sensitive data outward (transparency); it is not a citizen data-protection law — that is the DPDP Act, covered separately. The Gujarat IT/ITeS Policy 2022-27 is a state policy — its incentives (capital subsidy, GIDC/IT-park support, single-window clearance) apply to Gujarat specifically and sit alongside, not instead of, national schemes. Don't confuse "Global Capability Centre (GCC)" incentives with generic IT/ITeS incentives — the policy calls GCCs and data centres out as distinct, specially-incentivized categories.

Practice MCQs

Q1. Digital India's three declared vision areas are:

  1. Only expanding digital infrastructure as a core utility, with no citizen-facing services component included at all
  2. Digital infrastructure as a core utility, governance & services on demand, and digital empowerment of citizens
  3. Only promoting e-commerce activity for private companies, with no direct government service-delivery component
  4. Cybersecurity, national defence and foreign-policy coordination exclusively, with no domestic service-delivery focus
Show answer

Answer: B. These three vision areas are the officially stated pillars framing the Digital India program.

Q2. The National Data Sharing and Accessibility Policy (NDSAP) primarily addresses:

  1. How private citizens' personal data must be protected by companies that collect or process it under a formal consent framework
  2. How government departments proactively share the non-sensitive data they hold, to promote transparency and enable data-driven applications (e.g. via Open Government Data)
  3. Criminal penalties and enforcement procedures for cybercrime offences committed against government IT systems specifically
  4. Only how Aadhaar-specific identity data may be used for targeted delivery of government subsidies and benefits
Show answer

Answer: B. NDSAP is about government data-sharing/openness, distinct from citizen data-protection legislation like the DPDP Act.

Q3. Among the stated objectives of Gujarat's IT/ITeS Policy 2022-27 is:

  1. Discouraging any new IT investment in the state in favour of prioritizing traditional manufacturing sectors instead
  2. Attracting fresh IT/ITeS investment, generating skilled employment, and positioning Gujarat as a preferred hub for IT/ITeS, GCCs, data centres and emerging technologies
  3. Restricting IT companies to setting up operations only within one specific city, rather than the state as a whole
  4. Eliminating single-window clearance for new companies, requiring separate approval from every department involved
Show answer

Answer: B. Investment attraction, employment generation, and positioning Gujarat as a preferred IT/ITeS/GCC/data-centre hub are the policy's central objectives.

Q4. Which incentive category is specifically called out for companies establishing data centres or Global Capability Centres under Gujarat's IT/ITeS Policy 2022-27?

  1. No special incentive exists for these categories, since they are treated identically to any general IT/ITeS applicant
  2. Special incentives distinct from the general capital subsidy, reflecting the larger investment and strategic priority these categories represent
  3. A ban on data centres operating anywhere within the state, regardless of the scale of investment proposed
  4. A requirement that all data centres and GCCs be entirely foreign-owned before qualifying for any incentive at all
Show answer

Answer: B. The policy carves out dedicated special incentives for data centres and GCCs given their scale and strategic importance.

Q5. "Single-window clearance", offered under Gujarat's IT/ITeS Policy 2022-27, primarily benefits investors by:

  1. Requiring them to separately approach every relevant department for land, power and registration approvals, one at a time
  2. Letting them route approvals through one consolidated nodal process instead of navigating multiple departments separately, speeding up project setup
  3. Eliminating the need for any regulatory approval whatsoever before a company can begin operating in the state
  4. Applying only to companies that are legally headquartered outside Gujarat's own state boundaries
Show answer

Answer: B. Single-window clearance consolidates multi-department approvals into one streamlined process, reducing setup delays for investors.
